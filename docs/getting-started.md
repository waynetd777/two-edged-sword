## Start here

Two minutes to find your way around.

### Welcome

Two-edged Sword is for reading the Bible with everything written about it beside the text: commentaries, dictionaries, the Greek and Hebrew, other translations, and your own notes.

It comes with the KJV and some twenty more Bibles, Strong's dictionaries and two lexicons, the Treasury of Scripture Knowledge, commentaries from Matthew Henry to Keil & Delitzsch, a shelf of dictionaries, fifteen Bible maps and a shelf of books, so it works with nothing else installed. The [Library guide](library.md#built-in-modules) lists them all.

<!-- mac -->
If you have e-Sword X, the app reads its library too. Nothing in it is copied or changed.
<!-- /mac -->

### Allow what the app asks for

<!-- mac -->
macOS asks once for each of these. Allow them as they come:

| Prompt | When | For |
|---|---|---|
| Access data from other apps | First launch, if e-Sword X is installed | Reading e-Sword X's library |
| Documents folder | First journal save | The journal, in Documents › Two-edged Sword |
| Notifications | Turning on the daily reminder | The reminder |
| Control Music | First worship song | Worship music |

Refused one by mistake? Allow the app in System Settings › Privacy & Security.
<!-- /mac -->
<!-- windows -->
Nothing to allow: the app asks for no permissions. Your settings are kept in `%APPDATA%\Two-edged Sword` and your journal in Documents › Two-edged Sword.
<!-- /windows -->

### Choose your Bible

Settings › Bibles sets:

- the translation the app opens with;
- up to three favourites, each a button in the top bar;
- the Bibles Compare opens with.

The Bible menu in the top bar changes the translation until the app is next opened; then your default comes back.

### Move between screens

The sidebar has the eight screens:

| Keys | Screen |
|---|---|
| ⌘1 | [Read](app:read) |
| ⌘2 | [Compare](app:compare) |
| ⌘3 | [Search](app:search) |
| ⌘4 | [Word Study](app:word) |
| ⌘5 | [Journal](app:journal) |
| ⌘6 | [Quiet time](app:plans) |
| ⌘7 | [Library](app:library) |
| ⌘8 | [KJV History](app:history) |

Under them are your bookmarks and recent chapters. Hover one for a preview; × removes it.

### Go anywhere with ⌘K

Press ⌘K and type a reference ("rom 8 28"), a word, a Strong's number ("G26") or a command, then ↩.

[Try ⌘K](app:palette)

⌘[ and ⌘] go back and forward through screens, passages, words and searches, as in a browser.

### Get help anywhere

- Press ? on any screen, or click **?** at the top right, for that screen's help. Type in it to search all the help.
- Under the search box, **Getting started** brings back these walk-throughs and **All topics** lists every guide.
- Hover any button to see what it does.
- **Ask about Two-edged Sword**, at the foot of the help, asks the AI assistant how to do something.
<!-- mac -->
- Closing the window leaves the app in the menu bar, with today's reading. Quit from its menu, or with ⌘Q.
<!-- /mac -->
<!-- windows -->
- Closing the window leaves the app in the notification area, by the clock, with today's reading. Quit from its menu.
<!-- /windows -->

## Read the Bible

The reader, and the ways to move around and make it comfortable.

### Open a passage

[Open John 3](app:open/john-3)

Or click the passage picker at the top left and choose book, chapter and verse, or press ⌘K and type it.

← and → turn the page. Hover a reference anywhere for a preview of the verse.

### Verse or paragraph

The **Paragraph** and **Verse** switch at the top of the chapter changes the layout:

- **Paragraph** reads like a printed Bible.
- **Verse** puts each verse on its own line, with room in the margin for marks such as ≠ and your notes.

### Make it comfortable

- **Aa** in the top bar, or ⌘+ and ⌘−, makes the text bigger or smaller.
- Settings › Appearance sets the theme (light, dark or the system's), the font, and the words of Jesus in red.
- ⌘\ hides or shows the study pane.

### Focus mode

⌘. fills most of the window with the text alone, justified. The pointer hides when the mouse is still. Esc leaves focus mode.

While Read or Compare is on screen, all through Quiet time, and while anything is read aloud, the screen doesn't sleep or lock, in focus mode or not.

### Copy a passage

Select verses (click one, ⇧-click another for a range) and press ⌘C. The # button on the toolbar above them chooses whether verse numbers are copied too.

## Study a verse

The study pane beside the chapter follows the verse you select.

### Select a verse

[Open Romans 8](app:open/romans-8)

Click a verse (not a word) to select it; ⇧-click selects a range. The study pane on the right shows what's written about it.

### Commentaries and cross-references

The **Commentary** tab shows every commentary on the verse, one after another. Below them are the verse's cross-references: click **TSK** for those from the Treasury of Scripture Knowledge, or **OpenBible** for those from OpenBible.info.

- Choose which come first in the [Library](app:library#commentaries).
- Hover a reference for the verse; click it to go there.

### Dictionaries

Type a word or name in the **Dictionary** tab's box to look it up in all your dictionaries at once.

### Your notes and maps

- **Notes**: your journal entries on the verse.
- **Maps**: maps that suit the book you're in, from the built-in Bible Maps and your own map books. Click a map to see it enlarged.
- **ⓘ**: about the Bible you're reading: its edition, source and terms. Beside a book, it's about the book.

### Ask about it

The **Ask** tab answers questions about the verse or chapter from your own commentaries and dictionaries. It needs an AI tool first: see the Ask about Scripture walk-through.

## Greek and Hebrew

The original words behind any verse, without knowing the languages.

### Look up a word

[Open John 3](app:open/john-3)

Click any word in the text. A box shows the Greek or Hebrew behind it, its Strong's number, how the KJV translates it elsewhere, and dictionary articles. Its speaker button says the word aloud. Click a word in a commentary, dictionary or book to do the same.

### Word Study

Click **Word study** in that box, or press ⌘4, for everything about one word: its Strong's entry, every verse that uses it, a chart of where it's used, related words, and articles on it in your library.

[Open Word Study](app:word)

### Word-by-word Bibles

Bibles with + in their name, such as KJV+, carry Strong's numbers: hover a number for its entry.

Original-language Bibles with a +, such as these, show each word over its English. Hover a word for its grammar.

- WLC+ (Hebrew) and Byz+ (Greek)
- LXX-Brenton+ (the Septuagint)
- Vulg-C+ (the Latin Vulgate)
- Peshitta+ (the Syriac)
- Targum+ and Ps-Jon+ (the Aramaic Targums)

All of these come with the app.

Some original-language Bibles have Strong's numbers but no English of their own. For these, Settings › Appearance can show the English the KJV most often uses for each word.

### Search by Strong's number

Search for `G26` or `H430` to find every verse that uses that word, however it's translated.

[Open Search](app:search)

## Compare translations

Several translations side by side, verse by verse.

### Open Compare

[Open Compare](app:compare)

It opens at the passage you're reading, in the Bibles chosen in Settings › Bibles. To compare just some verses, select them in [Read](app:read) and click the compare button on their toolbar.

### Choose the columns

**Add** puts another translation alongside; × on a column removes it.

### See what's different

- **Highlight differences** marks the wording that differs from the first column.
- **Strong's numbers** shows the numbers in the Bibles that carry them.

← and → turn the page, as in [Read](app:read).

## Search

One search across a Bible, every commentary and dictionary, your books and devotionals, and your journal.

### Search for something

[Open Search](app:search)

Type a word or phrase and press ↩. **Look in**, down the side, shows results from everything, or from just the Bible, commentaries, dictionaries, books or your journal. Click a book's result to see the paragraph, and **Open** to read on from there.

### Narrow it down

- **Range**: the whole Bible, a Testament, or a part such as the Gospels or the Letters. Click the chosen one again to go back to the whole Bible.
- **Match**: the exact phrase, all of the words, or any of them.
- **Whole words only**: with this off, "tithe" also finds "tithes" and "tithed".

Hyphens inside words don't matter: "Bethel" finds the KJV's "Beth-el".

### Read results in context

Select a result to see it in context on the right; **Open** goes to it. The **Ask** panel below the results can summarise what they say.

## Highlights, bookmarks and notes

Mark what matters as you read.

### Highlight

Select a verse and pick one of the eight colours on the toolbar above it.

Give each colour a name in Settings › Highlights, such as "promises" or "commands". The colour pickers then show the name, and the journal offers it as a tag.

The highlighter button in the top bar hides and shows all highlights.

### Bookmark

The bookmark button on the same toolbar keeps the verse in the sidebar, under Bookmarks. Paragraphs in books can be bookmarked too.

### Add a note

Select a verse and press N, or click the note button on its toolbar. A journal entry starts, linked to the verse, and the verse gets a mark in the margin. The journal walk-through has more.

## Listen

The Bible, books and your journal, read aloud.

### Play a chapter

[Open Psalm 23](app:open/psalm-23)

Click the speaker in the top bar, or press ⌘P. Each word lights up as it's spoken, and reading carries on into the next chapter. To start at a verse, select it and click the listen button on its toolbar.

### The player

| Keys | Does |
|---|---|
| Space or ⌘P | Play and pause |
| F7 and F9 | Previous or next chapter |
| Esc | Close the player |

The player sets the speed (0.5× to 2×) and a sleep timer. Media keys and headphone buttons work too.

### Better voices

<!-- mac -->
The Premium voices sound far more natural:

1. Open System Settings › Accessibility › Spoken Content › System voice › Manage Voices.
2. Under English, download a voice marked (Premium), such as Zoe or Ava (US), or Jamie (UK).
3. Choose it in the player's voice menu or in Settings › Listening.

Hebrew, Greek and Latin Bibles use a Hebrew, Greek or Italian voice; better ones download the same way.
<!-- /mac -->
<!-- windows -->
1. Open Windows Settings › Time & language › Speech, and click **Add voices**.
2. Add the voices you want: Asaf for Hebrew, Stefanos for Greek, Elsa or Cosimo (Italian) for Latin Bibles, and other English ones such as Hazel or George.
3. Restart the app, then choose them in Settings › Listening.

Windows' "natural" voices are only for Narrator, so the app can't use them.
<!-- /windows -->

## Books, devotionals and maps

Your reference books and devotionals open in the reader, with the same tools as the Bible.

### Open a book

Click **Books** beside the Bible menu and choose one. Chapters are down the side; a devotional opens on today's reading.

### Use the same tools

Click a paragraph to highlight, bookmark, note, listen to, ask about or copy it, as with a verse. Click a word to look it up; hover a reference to preview it.

### Pictures and charts

Click a picture or chart to enlarge it:

- Scroll, or use two fingers on a trackpad, to zoom in and out.
- Drag to move it around.
- Double-click to fit it again; ← and → go to the chapter's other pictures; Esc closes.

### Maps

- Bible Maps comes with the app. Other map books open from **Books**, like any book.
- The study pane's **Maps** tab picks maps that suit the Bible book you're reading. Click one to enlarge it, as with pictures.
- On a Bible Maps map, click a place's dot or name for a card of its verses. This works in the book, in the Maps tab and enlarged. Point at a verse to read it; click it to open it.

## Keep a journal

Your notes on Scripture, linked to the verses they're about.

### Start an entry

[Open the Journal](app:journal)

⌘N starts a dated entry. To start one linked to a verse, select the verse in [Read](app:read) and press N.

### Write

The toolbar has headings, bold, italic, lists and quotes. It can also highlight text, insert or link a verse, and add a tag.

- Type a reference such as "Rom 8:28" and it becomes a link. Hover it for the verse.
- Type # to add a tag: your highlight themes first, then the tags you use most.
- ⌘F finds in the entry; ⌥⌘F replaces.

### Find your entries

Type in the box above the list to filter by words, tags or verses, or click a tag under the box. In [Read](app:read), each entry shows in the study pane's Notes tab when you select one of its verses.

### Listen, focus and ask

- **Listen** reads the entry aloud.
- ⌘. hides everything but the entry.
- **Ask** answers about this entry (select text first to ask about just that) or the whole journal: "What themes keep coming back?"

### Where it's kept

Entries are Markdown files, one per month, in Documents › Two-edged Sword. To use them in a notes app that works with Markdown files, such as [Brainstead](https://brainstead.davies.co.za/), choose one of its folders in Settings › Journal. Changes made there show in the app within a few seconds.

## Quiet time

A daily reading plan, read or heard, with devotionals.

### Choose a plan

[Open Quiet time](app:plans)

Pick one: the Bible in a year, the New Testament in 90 days, the Gospels, F. B. Meyer's daily readings, a Psalm and a Proverb a day, or your own.

### Add devotionals

A plan can include devotionals from your library, or Our Daily Bread and Heartlight from the web. **Add a website…** adds a devotional site of your own.

### Read each day

- **Read** opens each of the day's readings in turn; **Read with audio** reads them aloud, one after another.
- Parts are ticked off as you finish them. Your streak and the last seven days are under the progress bar.
- The calendar marks the days you read; click a day to read it.

F7 and F9 move between parts; F8 or ⌘P pauses.

### The daily reminder

Settings › Quiet time can remind you at a time you choose, if you haven't read yet that day. The notification lists the day's readings.

<!-- mac -->
Turning it on sends one straight away, so macOS can ask you to allow them. The reminder only comes while the app is running, even if it's only in the menu bar. To keep it running, turn on **Open at login** in the menu-bar window.
<!-- /mac -->
<!-- windows -->
The reminder only comes while the app is running, even if it's only in the notification area. To keep it running, click its icon there and turn on **Open at login**.
<!-- /windows -->

### End the day well

In a plan's page:

- **Closing verse**: the AI assistant picks a verse that gathers up what the day's reading taught, read at the end.
- **Favourite passage**: one of your own, such as Proverbs 3:5–6, read every day at the very end.

**Try it now** runs today's Quiet time without marking anything read.

## Worship music

Songs from your own music, chosen to suit what you're reading.

### Worship in Quiet time

[Open Quiet time](app:plans)

Turn on **Songs before the reading** or **Songs after the reading** in a plan's page. The AI assistant picks songs to suit the day's readings, and a card says why each was chosen.

<!-- mac -->
Songs play in Apple Music from your Music library. The first time, macOS asks whether the app may control Music.
<!-- /mac -->
<!-- windows -->
Songs are the music files in your Music folder (MP3, M4A, FLAC and others), and the app plays them itself. Only songs whose genre is Christian, Gospel or Worship are chosen: set it in a file's Properties › Details if it isn't.
<!-- /windows -->

### Words on screen

While a song plays, its words show in the reading column, with pictures behind them. When timed words can be found, they follow the music. Settings › Listening turns the pictures and their verses off.

A song isn't chosen again for 14 days.

### Songs for any chapter

The ♫ button in the top bar picks songs for the chapter you're reading, in the Bible or a book. Choose how many, click **Choose songs**, then **Play songs**. **Choose again** picks others.

## Ask about Scripture

Questions answered from your own library, by an AI tool you already use.

### Install an AI tool

Ask runs Claude Code, Codex, Antigravity or GitHub Copilot, installed and signed in with your own account.

<!-- mac -->
In Terminal, install one and sign in, for example:

| Tool | Install | Sign in |
|---|---|---|
| Claude Code | `curl -fsSL https://claude.ai/install.sh \| bash` | `claude` |
| Codex | `npm install -g @openai/codex` | `codex login` |
| GitHub Copilot | `npm install -g @github/copilot` | `copilot`, then `/login` |
<!-- /mac -->
<!-- windows -->
In PowerShell, install one and sign in:

| Tool | Install | Sign in |
|---|---|---|
| Claude Code | `irm https://claude.ai/install.ps1 \| iex` | `claude` |
| Antigravity | `irm https://antigravity.google/cli/install.ps1 \| iex` | `agy` |
| Codex | `npm install -g @openai/codex` | `codex login` |
| GitHub Copilot | `npm install -g @github/copilot` | `copilot`, then `/login` |

A terminal opened before the install may not find the tool: sign out of Windows and back in.
<!-- /windows -->

Then open Settings (⌘,): the app looks for the tool again, and Ask appears. Settings › AI assistant shows which tools were found.

### Ask about a passage

[Open 1 John 1](app:open/1-john-1)

Select a verse and open the study pane's **Ask** tab, or click the ask button on the verse's toolbar. Try "How do the commentators differ on this verse?" It reads your commentaries, lexicons and dictionaries, and names its sources.

Ask is also in Search, the journal and books, where it asks about what's on screen.

### Choose a model

The menu on any Ask panel chooses the model, such as Claude Sonnet or Opus; your choice becomes the default. After an answer, a follow-up question is suggested in grey: press → or Tab to use it.

### Keep the answers

Chats stay on your computer, under **Recent** on any Ask panel. **Add to journal** under an answer saves it as a journal entry.

### What is sent

Your first question is sent with what you're looking at. After that, only what the assistant chooses to read is sent. In Settings › AI assistant you can let it read your journal, limit it to the passage, or keep copyrighted modules' text back.

## Translations and manuscripts

Where translations differ, and where the KJV came from.

### Differences from the KJV

In Verse layout, a **≠** beside a verse marks where a translation such as the NIV or ESV differs in meaning from the KJV. Click it for both readings, what changed, and why.

### A Bible's own notes

Some Bibles, such as the ESV+, carry notes. In the text, small blue letters are cross-references and small numbers are footnotes: click one to read it.

### KJV History

[Open KJV History](app:history)

The KJV's family tree: the manuscripts, the printed editions the translators used, the English Bibles before it, its revisions since, and the Bibles translated afresh.

- Each card's chips show what you have; click one to read it.
- The ⓘ on a card says what the source is and what it gave the KJV.
- Hover a card to light up what it came from and what came from it.

### Targums and Talmud

The Aramaic Targums (ancient paraphrases of the Old Testament) come with the app; an English Targum and the Babylonian Talmud can be built from Sefaria. The Targums compare beside the KJV verse for verse, and Ask finds the Talmud passages that cite a verse.

## Add modules

Bring more Bibles, commentaries and books into the app.

### Your library

[Open the Library](app:library)

It lists every module you have, with its size and features. The ⓘ beside one shows its description and where it came from. **In picker** chooses which Bibles are in the Bible menu.

### Add a module

The app reads e-Sword X's Mac formats: `.bbli` Bibles, `.cmti` commentaries, `.dcti` dictionaries, `.lexi` lexicons, `.refi` books and `.devi` devotionals.

1. [Open your modules folder](app:modules).
2. Copy the module files into it, not into a folder inside it.
3. [Rescan](app:rescan), and they appear in the Library and the menus.

<!-- mac -->
### e-Sword X's library

With e-Sword X installed, modules from its Download window appear here too, while **Read e-Sword X** is on in the Library.
<!-- /mac -->
<!-- windows -->
### e-Sword's own modules

e-Sword for Windows' modules (`.bblx` and so on) can't be read: their text is encrypted. Use the Mac versions of the same modules.
<!-- /windows -->

### Find more

The Library guide lists [free modules worth having](library.md#free-modules-worth-having), and how to [build](library.md#building-modules) the Targums, the Talmud, Beza's Greek New Testament and others from free sources.

### Order and defaults

In the Library, set the default Bible, and the order in which [commentaries](app:library#commentaries) and [dictionaries](app:library#dictionaries) appear in the study pane.

## Make it yours

Settings, and keeping the app up to date.

### Settings

Settings (⌘,) has a section for each part of the app:

- **[Appearance](app:settings#appearance)**: theme, font and size, words of Jesus in red, verse or paragraph layout.
- **[Bibles](app:settings#bibles)**: the default, favourites and the Compare columns.
- **[Highlights](app:settings#highlights)**: a name for each colour.
- **[Listening](app:settings#listening)**: voices, speed, word highlighting, and the pictures behind songs.
- **[Journal](app:settings#journal)**: its folder, notes beside verses, and grammar checking.
- **[Quiet time](app:settings#quiet-time)**: the reminder, and what to do when you fall behind.
- **[AI assistant](app:settings#ai-assistant)**: the tools found, the default model, and what Ask may read and send.

### Updates

The app checks for a new version once a day. It shows what's new and asks before installing. **Install and Restart** saves your work first.

<!-- mac -->
To check now, choose **Check for Updates…** in the app menu or at the foot of Settings.
<!-- /mac -->
<!-- windows -->
To check now, choose **Check for Updates…** in the notification-area menu or at the foot of Settings.
<!-- /windows -->
